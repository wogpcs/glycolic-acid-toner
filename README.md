## What you're actually buying

Glycolic acid is an alpha-hydroxy acid — the smallest of the common AHAs, which is why it penetrates faster than lactic or mandelic acid and why a 7% leave-on toner is treated as a mid-strength product rather than a gentle one. The Ordinary's version is a water-based exfoliating toner, sold in a **240ml (8 fl oz) bottle**, and the brand positions it for both face and scalp. Scalp use is the reason this bottle went viral on TikTok, and it's also why it shows up in a dedicated hair collection on the brand's own site.

The format matters more than the marketing. A 240ml toner at this concentration lasts a long time if you're using it two or three times a week, which is why the price-per-use maths is more interesting than the sticker price.

## Using the toner without wrecking your skin

The product is popular enough that "how often" is a genuine search question, and the honest answer is: less often than the internet suggests.

A few things worth knowing before you commit:

- **Patch test first.** 7% glycolic acid is a real active. Two or three nights on a small area tells you more than any review.
- **Start at two or three nights a week**, not nightly. Build up only if your skin is calm.
- **Apply to dry skin and don't layer it with other exfoliants or retinoids in the same session.** Doubling up is the fastest route to a wrecked barrier.
- **Sunscreen the next morning is non-negotiable.** Chemical exfoliation increases photosensitivity, and this is the part people skip.
- **Skip it on freshly shaved, irritated, or sunburned skin.** Stinging isn't a sign it's working.
- **On the scalp**, it's used for product buildup and dryness. Wet or dry application, and how long you leave it on, matter — follow the brand's own instructions rather than a TikTok clip.
- **Don't use it on the same night as a retinol** if you're new to either one. Pick one active, run it for a month, then reconsider.

Two bottles, used properly, will last most people the better part of a year. That's the argument for buying the 240ml once rather than hunting for a cheaper 30ml trial size.
